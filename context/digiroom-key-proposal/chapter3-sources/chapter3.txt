# Solution Overview
Digiroom KEY connects the moments when a household needs to decide what happens next. Know Your Fit helps buyers judge an xEV against their own routine and budget. Easy Service turns a workshop enquiry into an agreed job that the right person can approve. Your Next Toyota brings the inspected value of the existing car into a replacement deal the owner can complete. The three solutions give Digiroom a continuing role across purchase, ownership, and replacement, while branch advisers remain responsible for the commitments customers rely on.
The proposition builds on Auto2000’s catalogue, service network, sales relationships, and existing appraisal routes. Its contribution is to connect the customer’s preparation with the adviser’s next action: the household checklist travels to the demonstrator appointment, the accepted estimate travels to the workshop, and the inspected offer travels into settlement. Each journey ends with a recognisable commercial outcome. Technical architecture and the distinction between the demonstrated interface and production integrations appear in [A25] and [A26].
# 1. Know Your Fit
## Overview
An xEV purchase has to work for everyone who uses and pays for the car. A specification sheet can describe dimensions and efficiency without resolving whether the garage door opens comfortably, the family fits with its luggage, or charging works around an ordinary week. Know Your Fit gives the household a way to answer those questions before committing to a purchase. The driver explores the car, saves the questions that matter, and brings them into a confirmed drive. The buyer then reviews an adviser’s offer alongside the household’s running-cost estimate.
This approach follows the contrast in the interviews. Apartment charging already suited the Wuling driver, while the Atto driver changed transport on one urgent low-charge occasion. The Yaris driver valued fuel savings and described the effect of passenger load on a long trip. Those differences make the household’s routine a better starting point than a generic electric-car pitch. The persona and its evidence boundaries are set out in [A24].
## How It Works
### Household Fit Studio
The household starts with the model it is considering. In the 3D studio, it enters the garage dimensions, checks clearance, and explores the cabin arrangement for passengers, a child seat, or luggage. These checks turn a broad concern into something the family can discuss: whether the car can be parked, how the seats will be used, and which questions still need a physical check. The household saves the result in a visit brief rather than repeating the information when it meets the adviser. The garage and cabin screens appear in [A28].
@K03
### Price the Driving Week
Next, the household describes its usual driving week. A hybrid estimate uses distance, consumption, and fuel price. A battery-electric estimate adds charging consumption and the household’s expected mix of charging locations. The driver can change those inputs and see which part of the routine drives the estimate. Where charging is relevant, the brief also records a practical fallback for a disrupted trip; the map alone does not confirm that a plug will be available.
The weekly estimate answers a different question from vehicle finance. Know Your Fit therefore keeps it beside the adviser’s purchase terms, with cash due upfront, monthly instalments, tenor, and any other payment clearly separated. This prevents a lower upfront amount from being mistaken for a lower total commitment. Assumptions remain visible and editable, while a lender’s decision is recorded separately from the sales quote. The calculation fields and financing handoff are detailed in [A29].
### Confirmed Drive and 48-Hour Life Test
Once the family knows what it wants to check, it requests a standard test drive or the 48-Hour Life Test. The saved brief tells the adviser which model is needed and what the household wants to learn. The adviser reviews demonstrator availability and the proposed time before confirming the appointment. If the slot is blocked or the branch cannot provide that format, the household receives another time, branch, or standard-drive option. A submitted request remains a request until the branch accepts it.
For an eligible take-home drive, the family uses the car on its own roads and checks the routine it prepared: parking at home, travelling with passengers, and understanding fuel or charging requirements. Pickup and return remain explicit handovers. Sales records the vehicle condition and agreed use arrangements, then discusses the household’s results at return. Demonstrator allocation, eligibility, insurance, deposits if applicable, and incident handling form the operating requirements for this offer; the interface does not establish that these arrangements are already contracted. The confirmation workflow appears in [A30], with the eligibility exception in [A43].
@K15
### From Drive to Delivery
The adviser uses the completed brief and drive results to prepare a dated offer for the selected variant. The driver can share that offer with the household buyer, who sees the same model and payment terms. If the quote changes, the revised version returns for review before the purchase proceeds. The journey then records financing, the order, and delivery as separate stages, giving the family a reason to return when a decision or confirmation is ready. It also identifies whether this is the customer’s first Auto2000 purchase, independently of prior Toyota or xEV ownership. [A31]
## Why This Works
Know Your Fit brings digital preparation into a physical decision that Auto2000 can fulfil through its sales network. The studio makes household questions concrete; the drive answers what a screen cannot; the offer lets the buyer judge the actual commitment. Together, these steps address the fit, affordability, and demonstrator-confirmation causes in the issue tree. They also preserve the sales relationship that helped the interviewed Yaris household buy through Auto2000.
The standard drive remains useful for families whose questions can be answered quickly. The longer option serves households that need to experience a normal route or home parking. Offering both lets the adviser match the experience to the unresolved question instead of treating drive duration as a benefit in itself. The customer returns to Digiroom to review a confirmation, save results, or accept current terms, each tied to progress towards buying.
## Expected Impact
The intended outcome is an additional xEV delivery to a customer new to Auto2000. Qualified enquiries, confirmed drives, drive completion, issued offers, and deliveries show where the journey progresses or stops. Demonstrator days and direct cost per completed drive show the resources required to support the longer experience. These measures distinguish a useful drive from a request that never receives a vehicle. KPI definitions appear in [A40]; numerical targets and gross-profit projections belong in Chapter IV.
# 2. Easy Service
## Overview
An owner choosing a workshop is choosing a particular job, price, and visit. Familiarity with Auto2000 for scheduled maintenance can coexist with trust in an independent tyre specialist or another repairer. Easy Service competes for that next job by showing which outlet can do it, what the installed price includes, and when the car can be collected. It carries the agreement through reception, technical work, additional approval, and handover.
The service offer includes eligible hybrid and other xEV work as well as appropriate petrol jobs. Coverage is checked at work-item level, so an included service and a customer-paid addition remain distinguishable. For owners already heading to Auto2000, the same process protects the intended visit. Recovery of a job from an outside workshop is identified separately from retaining existing Auto2000 work. [A32]
## How It Works
### Choose the Outlet for the Job
The owner begins with a vehicle and a task, or describes a symptom that needs diagnosis. Easy Service compares eligible Auto2000 workshops, Auto2000-operated OtoXpert outlets, and qualifying Home Service. The offer identifies the operator, supported vehicle, included parts and labour, exclusions, and collection window. An oil-and-filter job can therefore take a different route from a recurring fault that needs a diagnostic technician.
This is where the access model changes the offer. Eligible OtoXpert routing removes many of the modelled oil-service gaps in Medan and Palembang, while Bandung retains most of the remainder. The map makes those alternatives inspectable, but a modelled route does not reserve workshop capacity. The outlet confirms the job and time before the owner relies on the appointment. Detailed network screens and eligibility rules appear in [A32].
### Prepare and Receive the Car
For predictable work, the adviser checks fitment, reserves the required parts, and confirms a slot and expected collection time. For an unresolved symptom, the first agreement defines the diagnostic work and its cost; a repair quote follows the inspection. The owner can enter through Digiroom, WhatsApp, phone, or a walk-in, with the adviser opening the same work record. This preserves the channels that already work while giving the customer a consistent place to review the job.
At arrival, the adviser acknowledges the car and carries the accepted request into the workshop. The technician sees the symptom, previous relevant work, and the scope already agreed. If the car cannot be handled as planned, the owner receives a revised time or another suitable option before waiting becomes an unexplained delay. The operational handoffs are shown in [A33].
@E14
### Put the Decision in Front of the Payer
Inspection may uncover additional work. The adviser explains the finding, distinguishes required work from appropriate deferrals, and issues a revised estimate with the installed total and timing. When the driver is not the payer, the current estimate can be shared with the authorised family member. That person approves the specific version, declines the additional item, or keeps the original scope. The workshop sees which decision applies before work changes.
Version control matters because an earlier approval cannot silently carry over to a higher price or different repair. The interface therefore directs an outdated approval link to the current estimate. Production access also needs an authenticated, limited view with expiry and revocation; those controls protect the household’s information without requiring the payer to receive unrelated vehicle or account details. The approval sequence appears in [A34], with the stale-link example in [A43].
@E21
### Close the Work and Preserve the History
At collection, the adviser checks completed items against the accepted scope, records any unresolved symptom, and issues the receipt. The record separates customer payment, covered work, and programme reimbursement. If an issue remains, the owner sees the next action and responsible adviser rather than treating a collection status as proof that every complaint was resolved. The vehicle history can later support another service decision or, with permission, an appraisal. [A35]
## Why This Works
Easy Service addresses the points where the evidence shows a visit can unravel. Reception records respond to the reported unacknowledged arrivals. Written scope and price help explain why a workshop recommends a particular repair. A payer decision responds to the household role split in the Yaris interview. Meanwhile, preparation and symptom continuity address the cost of returning because an item was forgotten or a fault remained unresolved.
The proposition also makes useful distinctions within the network. A nearby light-service outlet can be appropriate for one job while a general workshop remains important for diagnostics and repair. The owner receives an offer suited to the work, and the branch receives the information needed to fulfil it. Digiroom earns a return when the estimate changes, approval is needed, or the completed record is ready; WhatsApp remains a practical route into that process.
## Expected Impact
The commercial outcome is additional completed customer-paid work, with jobs recovered from independent workshops reported as a distinct subset. Measures follow acknowledgement, confirmed readiness, authorised scope, completed work, and recognised contribution across channels. Rework, repeat visits for the same unresolved issue, payer disputes, and adviser time show whether the added coordination improves the visit. Covered service remains visible as an ownership outcome and is accounted for separately. [A40]
# 3. Your Next Toyota
## Overview
An owner researching resale prices is still several decisions away from buying the next car. A market range needs a buyer willing to make an inspected offer. The gross offer then needs to be reconciled with outstanding finance and fees, while the replacement must be available on terms the family can accept. Your Next Toyota brings these decisions into one upgrade journey, with an adviser coordinating the parties who control them.
The core offer is an inspection-backed replacement deal. The owner sees what the old car contributes, what cash remains due, and how the next xEV will be financed. A guaranteed future resale floor addresses a different risk and is outside this core offer. The Atto driver’s preference for certainty supports the importance of the question, but does not establish a funded guarantee or accepted purchase terms. [A11] [A23]
## How It Works
### Turn the Range into a Written Offer
The owner starts with the replacement model and requests an inspection when trading in a car. Relevant service history and condition evidence are shared with permission. The appraiser identifies the evidence accepted, records the inspection, and issues a buy offer naming the buyer, conditions, amount, and expiry. This moves the household beyond a market estimate while making clear what can still change before acceptance. The inspection and offer screens appear in [A36].
The family can also buy again without a trade-in. That route keeps the replacement quote and financing steps without inventing an old-car contribution. Keeping both paths available makes repeat xEV purchase the commercial objective, with trade-in serving households that need it.
### Show Usable Equity and the Amount Due
The current lender supplies the official payoff amount and its validity. The adviser reconciles that figure with the inspected offer and applicable fees to calculate usable equity. A positive amount contributes to the replacement; a shortfall becomes an explicit funding requirement. The owner reviews that result beside the exact replacement quote, cash due, instalments, term, and any balloon payment.
The worked example illustrates the arithmetic: a Rp248 million buy offer, less Rp174 million payoff and Rp2.5 million fees, leaves Rp71.5 million. These are sample transaction terms, not a market valuation. Showing the components lets the owner understand why the trade-in price and the amount available for the next car differ. Calculation rules and zero-equity or shortfall cases appear in [A37].
@Y04
### Align the Offer with the Replacement
The upgrade adviser brings together the inspected offer, lender conditions, and reservation for the specific replacement unit. The family reviews a dated set of terms before accepting a deposit or payment obligation. Each party confirms the commitment it controls: the appraiser confirms the offer, the lender confirms finance, and sales confirms the unit. Their dates must support the same transaction.
If the offer expires, finance changes, or the unit becomes unavailable, the adviser returns revised terms to the owner. The record explains whether the family can pause, seek a different vehicle, or receive a refund under the disclosed terms. A customer should not have to infer a current deal from an old appraisal and a separate stock message. Owner review and exception states appear in [A38].
@Y06
### Settle and Hand Over Both Vehicles
After acceptance, the adviser coordinates custody of the old car, official lender settlement, customer credit or shortfall, and replacement delivery. The record reconciles the payments and handovers, including any deposit, fee, or refund. An accepted offer and a reserved car remain intermediate stages; completion is recorded when the replacement is delivered and the associated settlement is reconciled. The handover workflow and completed record appear in [A39].
## Why This Works
Your Next Toyota builds on appraisal functions customers can already encounter. It addresses the remaining question of whether the owner can use the offer to complete a purchase. The Yaris interview supplies an example of price research that stopped at a range, while the deposit and allocation accounts show how separate commitments can obstruct a deal. Connecting those steps gives the adviser a practical way to carry interest into a replacement decision.
It also creates useful reasons to return over a longer ownership cycle. The customer reviews an inspected offer, checks revised funding terms, accepts a current deal, and follows settlement. Relevant service evidence can enter the appraisal with permission, linking Easy Service to the upgrade journey. When repair and replacement are competing choices, the household chooses the appropriate path and finance counts the outcome that actually occurs.
## Expected Impact
The intended outcome is an additional repeat xEV delivery to an existing Auto2000 customer. Inspection attendance, written offers, accepted terms, confirmed reservations, reconciled settlements, and delivered replacements reveal where the journey succeeds or stalls. Expired offers, withdrawn units, refunds, and time to settlement reveal the cost of failed coordination. The KPI framework counts repeat deliveries separately from new-customer acquisition and follows recognised vehicle gross profit without counting the trade-in value as another new-car sale. [A40]
