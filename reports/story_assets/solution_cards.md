# Twenty-seven concepts, in plain English

These are alternatives and coordination or contract layers, not 27 independent needs or additive profit pools. Possible additions are unmeasured hypotheses. The user chooses; no winner or implementation is selected.

## 1. Vehicle and finance choices

### SOL-01 | A use-based vehicle decision brief

**Already exists.** Auto2000 already compares vehicles and finance costs; Toyota publishes service estimates with an older date.

**Possible addition.** Help a household compare the actual vehicle, budget, journeys and charging arrangement using clearly dated assumptions.

**What could kill it.** Price or stock decides the purchase, or existing advice already settles the choice.

### SOL-02 | Practical home-charging readiness

**Already exists.** PLN and qualified installers already support home charging; a nearby charger does not establish permission to use it.

**Possible addition.** Clarify parking rights, building permission, electrical capacity and vehicle compatibility for the customer’s actual charging arrangement.

**What could kill it.** Existing providers resolve readiness, public charging already fits, or the required permission is impossible.

### SOL-03 | Finance status and total obligations

**Already exists.** The finance simulator works before contact entry; SEVA already offers routes toward a lender’s approval decision.

**Possible addition.** Explain which amounts are estimates, what remains unapproved and the customer’s full obligations under an actual offer.

**What could kill it.** The customer already understands the offer, or affordability and underwriting determine the outcome.

### SOL-04 | Purchase versus subscription on full obligations

**Already exists.** Subscription and commercial rental programmes exist, with different included costs, usage rules and ownership obligations.

**Possible addition.** Compare eligible purchase and subscription contracts for the same use, including end-of-term costs and restrictions.

**What could kill it.** No comparable eligible contract exists, or existing quotes already make the choice clear.

## 2. Service work, access and parts

### SOL-05 | Understandable service work and bill changes

**Already exists.** Work quotations and service records may already exist; this research did not inspect private repair orders.

**Possible addition.** Make necessary work, provisional estimates, approved changes and the final bill understandable within the same service case.

**What could kill it.** Current explanations are sufficient, or the dispute concerns actual repair quality rather than unclear information.

### SOL-06 | An appointment commitment customers can interpret

**Already exists.** Booking and guest routes exist; a requested date can still require branch review and confirmation.

**Possible addition.** Make clear whether the customer has requested a visit, received acceptance or secured a specific service commitment.

**What could kill it.** Current confirmations already work, or missing technicians, parts and physical capacity remain the binding problem.

### SOL-07 | Visit preparation and conditional timing

**Already exists.** Current forms and partner instructions already request vehicle details, documents and other task-specific prerequisites.

**Possible addition.** Explain what must happen before a visit, inspection or advertised completion time becomes a realistic commitment.

**What could kill it.** Preparation failures are uncommon, existing advisers resolve them, or extra instructions only increase effort.

### SOL-08 | A service route matched to the vehicle and job

**Already exists.** Branch filters, guest booking, home service and body repair routes exist; economical workshops add another existing choice.

**Possible addition.** Clarify which available provider can handle this model and job, including specialist or lower-cost care when relevant.

**What could kill it.** Current routing already works, or reliable capability and availability information cannot be obtained.

### SOL-09 | Compatible parts with clear coverage consequences

**Already exists.** Official parts channels and economical T-OPT parts exist; model lists do not establish every variant’s fit or stock.

**Possible addition.** Explain the actual part’s compatibility, availability, price and any protection consequences before the customer approves work.

**What could kill it.** Existing parts advice suffices, or price and unavailable supply matter more than information.

## 3. Ownership records and protection

### SOL-10 | A vehicle-specific protection decision

**Already exists.** Toyota already offers several care and warranty programmes, each with its own eligibility and payment rules.

**Possible addition.** Show which benefits apply to this vehicle now, what expires next and what any paid continuation actually covers.

**What could kill it.** The customer already understands the benefits, is ineligible, or receives better value from existing alternatives.

### SOL-11 | Protection after an ownership or use change

**Already exists.** Toyota allows remaining warranty to continue for eligible later owners; other programmes have different transfer and use conditions.

**Possible addition.** Explain what a sale, shared use or commercial use changes under the actual contract and ownership record.

**What could kill it.** Existing ownership updates resolve the change, or the relevant contract imposes no consequential restriction.

### SOL-12 | A battery result explained in buyer terms

**Already exists.** Toyota offers eligible battery checks and digital certificates; a certificate does not automatically guarantee resale value.

**Possible addition.** Explain what a specific test measures, its date and limits, and how warranty or appraisal decisions remain separate.

**What could kill it.** The measure is unreliable, existing explanations suffice, or the result changes no meaningful decision.

### SOL-13 | A coherent ownership record across jobs

**Already exists.** mToyota advertises service history; Astra describes customer systems, but private record completeness was not tested.

**Possible addition.** Keep permitted vehicle information useful when the customer moves between service, protection and replacement decisions.

**What could kill it.** Existing records are adequate, information cannot be shared appropriately, or continuity changes no outcome.

## 4. Replacement value

### SOL-14 | Condition evidence accepted in an appraisal

**Already exists.** Digital certificates, used-car inspections and BEV trade-in selection exist; appraiser acceptance of each report remains unknown.

**Possible addition.** Determine whether a buyer would use trustworthy condition evidence when making a real inspection and purchase offer.

**What could kill it.** The buyer does not accept the evidence, or market price dominates any useful condition information.

### SOL-15 | Trade-in value with explicit uncertainty

**Already exists.** Trade-in routes already separate an initial estimate from inspection and a later offer; competing bids remain available.

**Possible addition.** Explain why an estimate can change, which conditions matter and what obligations remain at the actual sale.

**What could kill it.** The process is already understood, or dissatisfaction concerns the market price rather than uncertainty.

### SOL-16 | A capped replacement incentive

**Already exists.** Appraisals and trade-in allowances exist; no Auto2000 contract for this proposed extra credit was verified.

**Possible addition.** Offer a clearly limited replacement credit, with its cap understood as different from a guaranteed future value.

**What could kill it.** The subsidy mainly rewards customers who would return anyway, or its cost exceeds additional contribution.

### SOL-17 | A contractual minimum trade-in value

**Already exists.** Some manufacturers offer conditional resale guarantees; this does not establish an equivalent Auto2000 promise or funding.

**Possible addition.** Assess a genuine minimum-value commitment, including who owes the full shortfall and which conditions apply.

**What could kill it.** The guarantor cannot afford or reliably honor the obligation when vehicle prices fall together.

## 5. Channel and relationship

### SOL-18 | One clear route for each ecosystem job

**Already exists.** Digiroom, Toyota apps, advisers and partner services already provide overlapping routes for different ownership jobs.

**Possible addition.** Help customers reach the correct existing service and decision owner without assuming that every route needs consolidation.

**What could kill it.** Customers already find the right route, or clearer navigation cannot resolve the underlying constraint.

### SOL-19 | Customer context carried into assisted help

**Already exists.** Dealer customer systems and insurer claim channels already exist; shared ownership does not prove useful information transfer.

**Possible addition.** Carry permitted case details into assisted help, while lenders and insurers retain their own decisions.

**What could kill it.** Existing case owners already have the context, or consent and partner authority prevent a useful handoff.

### SOL-20 | Access that fits household or fleet roles

**Already exists.** Toyota already allows another person to deliver a vehicle; G-Fleet provides an existing fleet-monitoring service.

**Possible addition.** Clarify who can view, approve and carry out a task without assuming the driver owns the vehicle.

**What could kill it.** Existing representative processes suffice, or customer and employer permissions cannot support the proposed access.

### SOL-21 | Support timed to meaningful ownership jobs

**Already exists.** Service reminders and loyalty programmes already exist; low-use PHEVs can still have engine and fuel-care needs.

**Possible addition.** Link support to meaningful ownership jobs, including low-use care, without treating frequent app visits as value.

**What could kill it.** Current reminders already work, extra contact adds no value, or fewer messages would miss necessary care.

## 6. Emergency and connected use

### SOL-22 | Local emergency help with explicit scope

**Already exists.** OEM and insurer emergency services already exist, with different locations, hours, incident types and entitlement conditions.

**Possible addition.** Clarify who can actually dispatch suitable local help, what it covers and any customer charges.

**What could kill it.** Existing helplines resolve the task, or reliable recovery supply remains unavailable despite clearer information.

### SOL-23 | Mobility continuity during repair

**Already exists.** Some programmes already offer conditional transport or replacement vehicles; household backup cars and existing rentals are alternatives.

**Possible addition.** Address a specific essential-mobility shortfall during repair, with clear eligibility, available supply and responsibility for costs.

**What could kill it.** Existing alternatives already suffice, or the required replacement vehicle cannot be supplied economically.

### SOL-24 | Understandable vehicle-linked account setup

**Already exists.** OEM vehicle-linked services and dealer-assisted setup exist; this research did not authenticate accounts or install native apps.

**Possible addition.** Explain the correct vehicle link, ownership checks and permitted support route when setup does not work.

**What could kill it.** Current setup support succeeds, or the dealer lacks a useful authorized role in resolving the issue.

### SOL-25 | An informed connected-service renewal choice

**Already exists.** Connected services and renewal routes already exist; fleet and private-vehicle contracts can have different terms.

**Possible addition.** Make the actual renewal price, included functions and contract owner clear for this vehicle and customer.

**What could kill it.** The current renewal route already answers the question, or Auto2000 has no useful authorized role.

## 7. Insurance and risk transfer

### SOL-26 | A voluntary EV protection choice with real terms

**Already exists.** EV-specific insurance and claims channels exist; battery wear, manufacturing defects and resale losses are different risks.

**Possible addition.** Help customers compare voluntary cover using the actual vehicle, insured events, exclusions, deductibles and repair-network conditions.

**What could kill it.** Existing policy explanations suffice, the relevant risk is excluded, or no valid distribution arrangement exists.

### SOL-27 | Investigate B2B residual-risk transfer

**Already exists.** Some international insurers cover agreed future-value risks for businesses; an Indonesian syariah contract was not confirmed.

**Possible addition.** Assess whether an actual insurer would accept a defined part of the dealer’s promised future-value exposure.

**What could kill it.** No suitable local contract exists, premiums overwhelm the benefit, or uncovered obligations remain unaffordable.

## Separate research candidate

EXT-OPT01 | VIN-specific safety-campaign completion across custody changes

**Already exists.** Toyota already provides a VIN recall lookup, campaign guidance and dealer booking; missing Auto2000 capability was not established.

**Possible addition.** Clarify authorized campaign completion for a current owner or buyer when existing records do not resolve the question.

**What could kill it.** Existing processes already provide usable completion evidence, or no consequential unresolved job exists.

Separate from the 27 concepts; conditional research candidate, no winner or implementation selected.
