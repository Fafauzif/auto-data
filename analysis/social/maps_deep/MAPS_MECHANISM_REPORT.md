# What the Maps evidence changes in the Auto2000 decision

Research cutoff: supplied exports collected 14 November 2026. All customer accounts below are self-reported public reviews, not authenticated transactions. The analysis reads complete narratives, retains contrary cases and differentiates the sampling cohorts. Source-language text is preserved in `curated_maps_evidence.csv` and JSON; the report uses bounded paraphrases to avoid amplifying names or allegations.

## Evidence base and analytical unit

Five exports contain 8,385 review rows, 8,119 distinct review IDs and 37 distinct place IDs. The two earlier Jabodetabek exports contain 3,976 rows. Later four-market collections contribute 1,915 Auto2000 rows and 932 Shop & Drive rows, both restricted to publication since 2025. A separate 1,562-row lowest-ranking Auto2000 collection spans much older dates. There are 266 IDs shared between the later latest and lowest-ranking samples. Their overlapping memberships remain attached to each unique review.

Publication date is not proof of visit date. The latest collections are capped, outlet-selected convenience samples. The lowest-ranking collection is purposefully selected to expose failure modes. It must never be pooled with latest collections to estimate branch complaint prevalence or compared with latest-only Shop & Drive reviews as a star-rating contest. Thirty-four purposively selected reviews anchor this report. A case count is not a count of independently verified customers, causes, losses or paid work orders.

`branch_run_coverage.csv` gives branch-by-run sample sizes, date coverage and observed rating counts. These are descriptions of the exports only. Generic five-star compliments often identify no vehicle or job. Long positive reviews and same-adviser praise can be useful accounts but do not establish authenticity or independent confirmation. Similarly, a detailed negative review does not establish the truth of an allegation.

## 1. The first recoverable paid-service opportunity is sometimes already inside the branch

**Decision:** pilot an accountable arrival-to-acceptance process before buying more demand for the same branch.

M01 is unusually relevant to the paid-workshop prong. A Setiabudi reviewer, published 8 August 2026, says they brought a Veloz for paid service, waited without being received despite a quiet reception, and decided to reject the branch. The review explicitly states `saya mau service berbayar, bukan service gratis`. This does not verify an invoice, but it identifies intended paid work more directly than inferring payer status from vehicle age. M13 describes a Cilandak regular who continued servicing after free service ended, then reports that a same-day shockbreaker promise became next-day completion and states an intention to change workshops.

These accounts locate a leakage mechanism after intention and arrival. A discount, another educational article or a shorter drive does not resolve an unreceived arrival or a vehicle immobilized contrary to the promised completion time. The immediate intervention is a named reception owner, a visible accepted/rejected work decision and an early ability to choose another time or branch. The business measure should be completed paid jobs retained or recovered, not app clicks or incoming leads alone. Arrival records and work orders would be needed to measure the magnitude; the accounts already justify the choice of process to test.

**Counterexample:** M12 describes a Cilandak experience with organized booking, prior consultation on additional work, no surprise charges and follow-up. It shows what the desired promise looks like in a customer account. It does not prove the branch performs uniformly. M01 and M13 should therefore inform a failure-control design, not a blanket ranking of Setiabudi or Cilandak as poor outlets.

## 2. A booking can exist in two channels and still fail at the physical handoff

**Decision:** carry one accepted booking identifier through arrival, reception, work start and collection; separate requested appointments from branch-accepted commitments.

M02, published 27 June 2025 at Gatot Subroto, reports booking three days earlier through an unnamed app, reconfirming by WhatsApp, arriving before the booked time, then receiving a manual queue number and waiting without the expected treatment. M03, a 2026 Pancing review, describes a non-booked arrival whose registration plate allegedly failed to enter the queue. M04 reports that booking at Plaju produced no perceived difference from an earlier unbooked experience.

The cross-case mechanism is a break between intent capture and local operating control. It is wider than app adoption because the non-booked case can fail at the same registration step. Calling the unnamed app Digiroom would overstate the source; the data do not identify it. Treating a submitted slot as a fulfilled appointment would also overstate the funnel.

A workable booking commitment requires branch acceptance, defined scope or triage, reception recognition and a completion expectation. Where a disruption occurs, the customer needs an explicit revised choice before surrendering the vehicle. A queue tracker by itself is insufficient if stock, technician availability or accepted work are unresolved. Conversely, M12 and the older Pancing contrast M14 show accounts in which booking, estimates and replacement approval are described positively. The evidence supports transferring those controls, not assuming every branch lacks them.

## 3. Price resistance can be resistance to an expanding or disputed work scope

**Decision:** offer a clearly bounded base job, distinguish safety-critical findings from optional additions and obtain traceable approval before changing the scope.

M05, a historical Kertajaya account published in December 2024, combines disputed promotional information, alleged replacement without confirmation, payment waiting and uncertainty that an invoiced task was completed. M06, published in October 2026 at Plaju, explicitly says the problem is not the amount but how work and approval were communicated. M07 describes an Avanza 2015 brake complaint, a larger official replacement estimate and a later cheaper outside repair.

M07 cannot validate either diagnosis, current prices or safety equivalence. It is therefore an argument for a transparent diagnostic explanation and choice architecture, not a recommendation to reproduce the cheapest repair. When an owner cannot see why an assembly is required instead of a smaller repair, a premium price is interpreted through an unresolved technical disagreement. An indiscriminate coupon would leave that disagreement intact.

M14 is the necessary opposite case: a Pancing customer describes service as somewhat expensive while approving of estimates and being asked before parts replacement. This is evidence that perceived higher price and acceptance can coexist in an account. The commercial choice is to make the difference purchasable and intelligible: included parts and labour, exclusions, alternatives when technically safe, approval record and completion evidence. Each acceptance remains a job-level decision; a customer saying “second service” is not enough to classify a payment as outside entitlement.

## 4. Hospitality and workshop throughput are incomplete measures of the customer's finished job

**Decision:** evaluate resolution and the number of required customer journeys alongside speed and reception satisfaction.

M08 at Soekarno Hatta praises friendliness, timeliness and the waiting room, yet reports five unresolved visits and becoming unwilling to return. M09 reports that complaints recorded for a first service were forgotten and required a next-day return. The historical Plaju warranty narrative M10 describes repeated travel and eventual home fitting. Its claimed number of trips is not a verified work-order series, and the vehicle powertrain is unspecified. It nevertheless makes the customer's cost structure explicit: zero payment for a covered repair does not mean zero cost to the owner.

This distinction changes the geographical synthesis. A shorter route to a workshop is valuable only for a service endpoint that accepts and completes the relevant work. Requiring another trip can outweigh small modelled access differences. The appropriate response varies by job: pre-confirmed oil/filter stock and an acceptance slot for routine work; a named case owner, diagnostic history, parts readiness and resolution plan for a recurrent fault. A fast oil-service promise must not be generalized to uncertain diagnosis or warranty repair.

M12 describes a positive closed loop with approval and follow-up. That supports “job completed as agreed” as a coherent operational target. It does not establish that a new digital screen alone would change technician quality. Repeated unresolved faults need escalation and technical ownership, with the digital record carrying the case between teams.

## 5. The strongest hybrid showroom examples concern a household's constrained transaction, not a generic information gap

**Decision:** build a purchase case that can move asynchronously through product questions, finance, handover and documentation under one accountable adviser.

M15 describes a Yaris Cross HV purchase at Setiabudi by a household working in Jakarta and present in Bandung on weekends. The account explicitly values online handling and administrative follow-through that avoided repeated dealership trips. M16 describes a Zenix HV purchase with credit processing, handover and registration documents. These are specific hybrid purchase accounts, unlike an undifferentiated positive dealership rating. Neither establishes that the purchaser was new to Auto2000. Their close dates and praise for the same adviser also preclude treating them as independent validation of a branch-wide operating model.

M17 supplies a contrasting failure account. A Gatot Subroto visitor says a Toyota-versus-Honda comparison ended in a Honda purchase after receiving more informative and welcoming treatment elsewhere. The model, powertrain and purchase are not independently verified. The source still identifies the relevant interaction: a live shortlist was lost at the point where explanation and treatment should have converted uncertainty into a next step.

The implication is to define the customer's pending decision and maintain it through human contact, rather than simply add more product content. M18 says “first Toyota purchase” at Ahmad Yani but supplies no powertrain. It must not become a first-time xEV conversion statistic. Prong 1 reporting needs separate fields for first Auto2000 purchase, first Toyota purchase, first xEV purchase and current stage, leaving unknown values unknown.

## 6. Appraisal novelty now has a direct local counterexample; the harder gap is an executable, reconciled transaction

**Decision:** prioritize the transition from indicative valuation to an accepted offer, net upgrade amount, reserved replacement unit and completed settlement.

M19 and M20, both published 12 October 2026 at Kertajaya, describe a TRIVA appraisal application. The second mentions photographs and vehicle data for an Xpander, an estimated value, OLX comparisons and service scheduling. These are two same-day customer-review claims, not official capability documentation or proof of a public production service. They are strong enough to challenge a pitch that treats an appraisal interface as absent and therefore novel. They do not demonstrate an accepted offer, trade-in settlement, battery-adjusted value or a repeat xEV purchase.

M21, a historical Gatot Subroto trade-in account, reports a trade-in application not being approved and difficulty recovering a supposedly returnable deposit. M22 alleges that an OLXmobbi Tamini unit was sold to another buyer despite a booking fee and credit approvals, followed by a delayed refund. Both are unverified accounts; neither identifies an xEV. They nevertheless expose the interdependence of valuation, credit, unit reservation and money movement. Improving a price estimate does not by itself protect those dependencies.

M23 is a positive OLXmobbi contrast in which an outside inspector allegedly agreed with the outlet description. M24 disputes condition and spare-key availability despite a claimed 188-point inspection. A certificate count is therefore a weak substitute for a transaction-specific accepted condition record and handover reconciliation. No battery-value premium should be inferred from these non-xEV accounts. The design implication for prong 3 is a reconciled deal file, not a new universal resale guarantee.

## 7. A battery rescue story is not proof that the same endpoint can fulfil an oil job

**Decision:** match routing and service promises to exact vehicle, job, stock, field-service capability and entitlement.

The four selected Shop & Drive collections include vivid battery replacement, home-service and warranty accounts. M27 at Pangeran Ayin describes an urgent morning no-start call followed by at-home battery replacement. M28 describes replacement on the final warranty day. M30 at MERR describes home replacement with no extra callout charge in that particular account. These support an urgent, portable battery job as a distinct customer proposition. They cannot establish mobile oil-service coverage, a current standard tariff or the economics of broad vehicle pickup.

M25 at Cibaduyut is more directly relevant to oil: it names engine and transmission oil, organized work, speed and perceived product authenticity. M26 at MERR supplies a useful scope boundary: a customer with an American vehicle reports incompatible filter stock and refusal to install a customer-supplied filter. That incident does not establish a Toyota fitment problem. It shows why a map category such as “oil-change service” does not settle the entire capability question.

M29 is a contrary MERR account describing failure to receive a warranty-required battery check. Even within one chain, the label and reputation do not ensure local execution. The geographic layer should therefore store a job-capability flag with evidence and date. When it is unknown, it must not silently become “capable”.

## Branch-specific bridge to the geographical decisions

The branch evidence differentiates the intervention rather than ranking outlets by rating. At Pancing, M03 locates a plate-registration failure before work acceptance; M14 provides a positive approval-and-estimate contrast. At Tanjung Api Api, historical M11 describes a booking not found on arrival and promised tyres not ordered, while M31 reports an Innova brake concern shortly after a brake check. The latter is a self-reported unresolved technical concern, not proof that the service caused a fault. Together these support named reception, case and release ownership when preserving general-workshop access.

At Soekarno Hatta, M08 and M09 support a one-visit resolution protocol, including preserved complaints, confirmed parts and a planned diagnostic escalation. At Ahmad Yani, M32 explicitly says a customer selected the branch instead of their regular outlet because of urgent toll-road access, then disputed communication and results for alignment/balancing and diesel-filter work. M33 is a contrary light-service account praising two-person work and readiness for a family event. The exact job and the promise kept matter more than assuming a branch-wide quality score from either story. M34 further shows a customer account with a paid diagnostic check followed by a warranty claim, so a single visit cannot safely be labelled wholly paid or wholly free.

These accounts belong in the geographical branch matrix as process evidence with provenance. Their hours of alleged delay must not be mechanically added to modelled OSRM travel times as if they were measured average service durations.

## 8. Use positive reviews to define the promise, not to erase operational leakage

Across the selected narratives, friendliness, refreshments and advisor praise coexist with both satisfactory and unsatisfactory outcomes. M08 is a within-review contradiction to a hospitality-only model. M12 and M14 describe concrete approval and completion practices, making them stronger process contrasts than an unspecified “good service”. M15 provides a specific household scheduling benefit; M28 gives a specific entitlement outcome. These can define the desired service contract without requiring a claim that all positive reviews are verified.

A practical branch dashboard should therefore distinguish: arrival received; requested appointment accepted; scope approved; parts and technician ready; job completed; customer collection completed; repeat visit for the same issue; documentation or refund still open. The present review corpus establishes examples of why those states matter. It does not supply their operational denominators. Star averages and sentiment totals cannot replace them.

## Evidence and reproduction

Run `python build_maps_deep.py --input PATH_TO_ORIGINAL_EXPORTS --output OUTPUT_DIRECTORY` with Python 3. Standard-library dependencies only. The script preserves original text with HTML tags removed and entities decoded, joins only on `reviewId`, records all source-file memberships and produces 34 curated cases. Full raw exports must remain available elsewhere in the repository or companion archive. It does not infer missing authors or merge people across platforms.

Case citations such as M01 resolve to `curated_maps_evidence.csv` or JSON, which include the exact original review ID, outlet place ID, publication date, rating, cohort, source filenames and interpretation. The selected quotes are illustrative mechanisms, not a representative qualitative saturation claim. This report deliberately makes no population complaint-rate, causal conversion-uplift or realized-GP claim.
