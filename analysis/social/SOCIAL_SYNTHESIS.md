# The opportunity is to complete a customer's decision, not accumulate more mentions

Research cutoff: 14 November 2026. This analysis covers every supplied X, Google Maps and YouTube export in the current upload collection. Original-language bodies remain available in the cleaned JSONL files and immutable source exports. The qualitative findings distinguish what an account says, what it can establish and what business choice follows. They do not authenticate authors, invoices or offline events.

## What the complete social collection actually contains

| Source family | Export rows | Unique platform IDs | Analytical role |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| X, ten exports | 14,892 | 14,291 | Discovery, stated choice, ownership narratives, advice, programme communication and substantial off-topic/commercial material |
| Google Maps, five exports | 8,385 | 8,119 | Named-outlet service and sales accounts, with separate latest and lowest-ranking selection cohorts |
| YouTube, one export across three videos | 74 | 74 | Selected comment discourse, replies and a few specific self-reports |

These are platform records, not 22,484 distinct customers. There is no attempt to link a person across platforms. Repeated review/post IDs are deduplicated within platform while all source-run memberships remain recorded. No body-text conflict was found among repeated IDs. Engagement is an export-time attribute and is not used as a population weight.

The Maps corpus has 5,504 unique reviews with non-empty text, across 37 place IDs. Its latest four-city collections comprise 1,915 Auto2000 and 932 Shop & Drive rows. The separate 1,562-row lowest-ranking collection overlaps those recent collections in 266 IDs and reaches back to 2011. Lowest-ranking reviews explain failure mechanisms; they are not combined with latest-only reviews to estimate complaint prevalence. Publication time is not necessarily visit time. Sixty-two reviews belong to a place marked permanently closed, B-Quik Lebak Bulus; they remain historical evidence rather than a currently available alternative.

The X collection spans 1 January 2025 to 13 November 2026 and contains 8,162 distinct author keys, 6,186 replies and 1,658 quote posts. The immediate parent is absent for 5,704 replies. A broad query can therefore return a highly specific-sounding fragment without the context needed to understand it. Language metadata labels 14,162 posts `in`, but this metadata is not an independently validated Indonesian-language filter. Broad-query output also contains motorcycle content, fiction, political discussion, unrelated long narratives, news and sales listings. The retrieval flags supplied here expose that mixture; they are not a validated sentiment or owner classifier.

All 37 unique X post bodies containing the case-insensitive string `digiroom` come from the named Auto2000 brand account, `AUTO2000ID_`. Those bodies contain 45 literal string occurrences, so 37 is a post count rather than an occurrence count. This makes X useful for auditing the brand promise, but provides no independent Digiroom-task account in this collection. It does not mean Digiroom has no users: the earlier repository already contains selected app reviews, including specific self-reported interactions. Neither evidence stream verifies a completed offline transaction merely because an app is mentioned.

## 1. A restoration job can retain an older Toyota while delaying the very replacement the sales prong wants

**Decision:** make retain, repair and replace explicit alternatives in one customer case, with separate economic outcomes.

SX01, post `1988319241666441699`, is a particularly useful expansion beyond oil-service commentary. A self-described used-Toyota owner reports buying a 2010 vehicle for Rp100m and spending Rp25m on Auto2000 suspension work plus an audio upgrade. The author compares this with a younger used vehicle around Rp120m and prefers the restored older car. The account also acknowledges a cheaper specialist route. The entire Rp25m is a bundled self-reported amount, not an Auto2000 invoice or a validated repair tariff.

This changes the paid-workshop opportunity. It can concern a meaningful restoration decision competing against a vehicle purchase, rather than simply moving an identical oil change between outlets. Yet a successful restoration can defer replacement. Counting the repair as both recovered paid work and an immediate xEV sale would create a contradiction in the business model. The appropriate recommendation is a staged condition-and-cost plan: diagnose the old Toyota, state the essential work and alternatives, then compare retaining it with an executable upgrade offer. Credit each completed outcome once, while retaining the customer relationship across the horizon.

SX10, a satisfied used-Nissan ownership account, is a useful adjacent counterexample rather than Toyota evidence. The writer accepts running costs for family space and imagines EV conversion in three to five years. Interest in electrification can coexist with a rational current decision to keep an ICE vehicle. A lead score that treats such discourse as near-term purchase demand would overstate acquisition potential.

## 2. The most direct paid-job leak is sometimes an unreceived arrival, not an absent lead

**Decision:** protect acceptance and completion before paying to create more demand for the same branch.

The Maps evidence provides unusually relevant intended-payer cases. Setiabudi M01 explicitly says the writer arrived for paid Veloz service and was left unreceived. Cilandak M13 describes continuing after free entitlement ended, then objecting when a same-day repair became next-day completion. Both are self-reports, but they identify the commercial boundary more directly than assuming an old vehicle must always be a paid job.

X adds a necessary rival view. SX03 describes choosing a workshop by routine service, body repair, parts or brand specialization. SX04 reports a community already supplying a workshop handoff during a breakdown. The existing discovery system is not empty; Auto2000 must win a specific accepted job against a credible alternative. The operational proposition should therefore be reception ownership, a clear accepted scope, readiness and an agreed completion or escalation path. Another discount or discovery screen would leave an unreceived arrival unchanged.

Use completed incremental paid work as the economic outcome. Covered, recall and warranty-recovery work require a separate case path. SX08, a publisher's allegation about an Altis recall visit, illustrates why a dissatisfied returning vehicle is not automatically a paid upsell opportunity. The business can protect trust without claiming immediate customer-paid GP.

## 3. The digital handoff has to commit a usable resource, not merely record a request

**Decision:** separate a requested test drive or service slot from a branch-accepted commitment and a completed visit.

SX02, post `2003711716526563346`, says the author wants to buy but cannot find an Auto2000 test-drive unit. The model and geography are not identified, so it cannot establish a national xEV demonstrator shortage. It does establish the kind of promise to test: a form submission is insufficient if no matched vehicle will be present.

Maps M02 describes an app booking, WhatsApp reconfirmation and arrival before the appointment, followed by a manual queue. The app is unnamed and must not be labelled Digiroom. M03 describes a Pancing registration failure even without booking, locating the problem partly in physical reception rather than app adoption. For prong 1, confirm the exact demonstrator, date and accountable adviser. For prong 2, confirm job scope, acceptance, required parts and realistic completion expectation. For both, preserve the same case identifier across channels.

Maps M15 and M16 provide positive hybrid purchase contrasts. The Setiabudi Yaris Cross HV account values remote handling for a household working in Jakarta and returning to Bandung on weekends; the Zenix HV account describes finance, handover and registration documents. These are concrete transaction benefits, not proof that buyers merely need more product information. Neither establishes first-time Auto2000 status. “First Toyota”, “first xEV” and “first Auto2000” must remain different fields.

## 4. A sole-car household can prefer operational resilience even while admiring a rival's technology

**Decision:** organize the demonstration and offer around the household's complete usage and fallback needs.

SX05, post `2067793260903645526`, praises competing technology while saying the author can maintain only one car and therefore still prefers a Japanese option. The competitor's precise model and feature assertions are not independently checked. The meaningful distinction is the household constraint. A family adding a second car can tolerate a different set of uncertainties from one replacing its sole vehicle.

The YouTube charging account SY02 makes the same issue concrete in another domain. The author says they use an EV without installing a supplied wall charger because a separate meter would cost about Rp6m, and instead use public charging every two weeks. Replies dispute or qualify that installation figure. No current PLN tariff can be derived from the thread. The purchase case should show the actual installation-inclusive household cash requirement and charging route, rather than declaring home charging universally present or absent. Toyota HEV, PHEV and BEV cases must not inherit one another's charging assumptions.

## 5. Resale concern combines condition, supply continuity and cash settlement; a new estimate screen solves only one component

**Decision:** prioritize an executable net-upgrade deal over a generic valuation interface or universal residual-value guarantee.

The merged X collection recovers the opening of the EV-saving thread previously visible only as a fragment. SX06, `2068876979131355331`, attributes the author's EV switch to Jakarta odd-even restrictions and increasing repair needs of a car over five years old. SX07, `2068881419091542064`, says savings are becoming visible and may be moved into a less liquid product for roughly five years as a personal future-value buffer. The immediate parent and intervening posts remain missing. The two records belong to one author/thread, not two confirmations. They demonstrate that an owner may respond to resale uncertainty through a personal saving strategy, not necessarily by demanding a manufacturer-funded price floor.

YouTube SY03 reports an Atto1 choice influenced by the producer's local factory commitment. SY06 offers dealer closure, rather than electric propulsion itself, as an explanation for the Neta resale story. These comments do not verify factory progress, parts availability or Neta's business status. They show that respondents distinguish continuity of support from battery condition and technology. The selected Neta video supplies 45 of the 74 comments; treating its comment section as the general EV market would mostly reproduce the video's framing.

Maps M19 and M20 add a direct local novelty challenge: two same-day Kertajaya reviews describe a TRIVA application with appraisal inputs, estimates, OLX comparisons and service scheduling. These are customer-review claims, not official product confirmation. They are sufficient to require a novelty check before pitching a generic appraisal UI as absent. Maps M21 and M22 instead expose dependencies among credit, deposit return and unit reservation, while M23 and M24 contrast accepted versus disputed condition evidence. The harder commercial bridge is an accepted offer, loan settlement, reserved replacement unit, reconciled handover and closed money movement.

## 6. A positive xEV remedy is more useful for service design than many generic warning comments

**Decision:** make symptom, responsible endpoint, remedy and closure searchable, while keeping technical claims out of the evidence model unless supported by appropriate sources.

YouTube SY01 describes M6 window-rubber noise reportedly ceasing after treatment at Harmoni Slipi. It is a specific positive self-report with a named endpoint and outcome, unlike generic allegations that aftersales is a gimmick. The contrast is operationally informative: a named case owner and a completed remedy can make the support promise tangible. It does not verify all repair capability at that endpoint or establish Toyota comparative quality.

Conversely, the comments asserting universal battery failure or harmful charging effects do not establish engineering facts. Their analytical role is perceived risk. They must not become modelled battery-replacement probabilities or charging advice. A digital case repository can distinguish customer concern from technical diagnosis and preserve both without confusing their evidential status.

## How the three prongs should use this evidence

| Prong | Primary decision supported | Measure to obtain operationally | Alternative that loses priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| New-to-Auto2000 xEV acquisition | A household-specific purchase case with a confirmed demonstration and accountable transaction owner | Qualified cases, matched demonstrator confirmation, attendance, first-Auto2000 status and completed delivery | Counting education views, social enthusiasm or a submitted form as converted buyers |
| Paid-workshop recovery | Job-specific routing and acceptance, bounded scope, readiness, authorization and resolution; restoration where relevant | Payer/entitlement by job line, accepted and completed work, same-fault returns and incremental paid contribution | Broad discounts or pickup that leave scope/acceptance failures intact |
| Repeat xEV replacement | Accepted condition evidence and executable net upgrade, including credit, reservation and settlement | Verified prior Auto2000 history, accepted offer, loan settlement, reserved replacement and completed exchange | A generic estimate UI or an unfunded universal resale guarantee |

The Maps mechanism report and geographic branch matrix should determine where each process is most relevant. Social narratives do not supply branch capacity, vehicle stock or demand weights for origin points. Modelled travel minutes must not be added to anecdotal hours of waiting as if both were measured averages.

## Reproduction and audit trail

`build_social.py --input-dir PATH_TO_ORIGINAL_EXPORTS --output-dir OUTPUT` rebuilds all three cleaned corpora, run membership, source hashes, branch/cohort coverage, YouTube video coverage and metrics using Python's standard library. `curate_social.py --social-dir OUTPUT --pilot-cases PATH_TO_PILOT_CASES_JSON` attaches explicit analyst judgments to stable IDs. `maps_deep/build_maps_deep.py --input PATH_TO_ORIGINAL_EXPORTS --output OUTPUT` rebuilds the 34 Maps cases and coverage tables.

The X/YouTube curated register has 45 analytical entries, including preserved pilot interpretations; repeated records can support more than one analytical entry. These are not 45 independent customers. Current parent/root coverage is stored alongside inherited pilot notes so a formerly missing root is not silently treated as still absent. Read the exact original record before reusing a case. The 34 Maps cases are purposefully chosen contrasts, not a saturation or prevalence claim.

The codebook documents all retrieval expressions, including their false-positive risks. Automated flag counts are useful for inspecting a broad corpus, not for declaring owner sentiment or sales demand. No paid Actor run, customer contact, CRM access, transaction authentication or new fieldwork was performed for this analysis.
